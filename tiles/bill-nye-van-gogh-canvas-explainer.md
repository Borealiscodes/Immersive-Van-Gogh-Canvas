## 🎨 Part 1: How This Interactive Proof of Concept Works

Even though the entire project fits in a handful of tiny files with zero external libraries, four real engineering systems are working together smoothly:

* **1. Touch-as-Piano Melody Sequencer (`gogh-audio.js`):**  
  Instead of playing a passive background MP3, every tap acts like pressing a piano key—advancing one note at a time through a G-major arrangement of *Vincent* (*"Starry, starry night..."*). Using a triangle wave plus 2nd and 3rd harmonic overtones with a fast `4 ms` hammer transient makes each note ring cleanly from pure math!
* **2. Rotational Vortex Flow Field (`gogh-kernel.js`):**  
  Each star you place acts like a gentle spinning whirlpool (`vortex curl`) in `Q16.16` fixed-point math. The `120` cobalt and cerulean brushstroke dashes bend smoothly around your stars as they drift across the sky.
* **3. Holistic 4-Stage Scene Evolution (`gogh-palette.js`):**  
  As you play through the verse (`0` → `28` notes), the painting grows naturally in four stages—from lone twilight stars, to swirling wind currents, to the glowing cadmium crescent moon and Saint-Rémy village skyline, and finally to the swaying foreground cypress tree.
* **4. Photosensitivity-Safe Steady Rendering (`gogh-renderer.js` & `style.css`):**  
  By painting an opaque twilight sky gradient and disabling mobile tap-highlight flashes, stars bloom gently like warm paint soaking into canvas—with zero harsh strobing.

## 🧪 Part 2: Why Tiny Proofs of Concept Lead to Great Canvases & Games Later

In software and game design, big ambitious projects almost always fail when people try to build everything at once. Small, working proofs of concept like this one are the secret weapon of great creators:

1. **You Catch Real Feel & Accessibility Issues Early:**  
   Testing a small prototype on a real tablet immediately revealed two crucial things you can't see on paper: mobile tap-flashes need to be disabled for visual comfort, and piano envelopes need a crisp `4 ms` attack to sound expressive. Fixing those in a 200-line prototype takes minutes instead of weeks!
2. **Proves the Core Toy Is Fun Before Adding Content:**  
   If tapping a blank screen to spawn a star and hear one piano note already feels peaceful and rewarding on its own, you know the core mechanic works before you ever spend months building levels, menus, or 3D assets.
3. **Reusable Building Blocks (`Ledger 1` / `Ledger 2`):**  
   Because the swirling vortex physics (`Ledger 1`) is cleanly separated from the WebAudio piano and Canvas renderer (`Ledger 2`), you can plug this exact same star-vortex engine into a full space exploration game, a rhythm puzzle game, or a multi-screen gallery installation later.
